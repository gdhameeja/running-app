# Bodyweight Strength and Plyometric Training for Recreational Runners (home, no weights, no pull-up bar)

Labels: **[Published]** = peer-reviewed study/meta-analysis; **[Coach/secondary]** = coach, commercial or community source. Most findings below come from abstracts and indexed summaries, not full texts. Where only a secondary source carried a number, it is flagged.

## 1. Does strength/plyometric training improve running economy (RE) and performance?

### Takeaway
Yes, moderately. Adding 2-3 strength sessions a week improves RE by roughly 2-8% in most trials. Heavy-load and combined (heavy + plyometric) methods give the clearest effect. Plyometrics alone give a small or trivial RE benefit, mostly at slower speeds. Submaximal-load and isometric-only training showed no significant RE benefit in the largest meta-analysis. VO2max is usually unchanged; the benefit is economy and neuromuscular.

### Cited Findings
- [Published] Blagrove, Howatson & Hayes 2018 (Sports Med 48:1117-1149) reviewed 24 studies of middle- and long-distance runners. RE was measured in 20 and generally improved by 2-8% versus controls, though not always. VO2max, vVO2max, lactate and body composition were typically unaffected. Time-trial and anaerobic speed measures tended to improve. Their recommendation is to add **2-3 strength sessions a week using a variety of strength modalities** — [PMC5889786](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5889786/)
- [Published] Llanos-Lagos et al. 2024 (Sports Med 54:895-932) studied 652 runners (195 moderately trained, 272 well-trained, 185 highly trained) in programmes of 6-24 weeks. Results by method:
  - High-load training gave a small RE improvement, and combined methods a moderate one.
  - Plyometrics gave a small RE improvement, only at speeds of 12 km/h or below.
  - **Submaximal-load and isometric training: no significant improvement versus controls.**
  - Gains from high-load training were larger at higher speeds and in runners with higher VO2max.
  - GRADE certainty was moderate for most methods and low for combined methods.
  - Sources: [UNAB repository](https://repositorio.unab.cl/items/13e3ac75-9905-4902-81f7-232752e84ae6/full), [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/37272/)
- [Published] Eihara et al. 2022 (Sports Med Open) compared add-ons to running training. Heavy resistance training improved RE more (g = -0.32) than plyometrics (g = -0.13), and the plyometric confidence interval crossed zero. Both should run for **10 weeks or more** — [PMC9653533](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9653533/)
- [Published] Dudagoitia Barrio et al. 2023 found a trivial pooled RE effect of plyometric jump training (ES = 0.19; 18 studies in the review, 10 in the meta-analysis). Larger gains came with **more than 15 total sessions, more than 7 weeks, and more than 2 sessions a week** — [UNAB repository](https://repositorio.unab.cl/items/2cf806a8-b611-4964-8dcb-f365dfa88ccc/full)
- [Published] Balsalobre-Fernández et al. 2016 found a large RE effect (SMD -1.42) in highly trained runners. The programmes mixed resistance and plyometric work — [PubMed 26694507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694507/)

### Inferences
- The two ingredients with evidence are **high force** (heavy or near-maximal effort) and **reactive/plyometric** work. A bodyweight plan should get both: hard single-leg or loaded-backpack variations, plus a plyometric block. That is the "combined" method, which had the largest effect.
- An app should expect RE benefits only after about 8-10 weeks and roughly 15 or more sessions. Consistency, not a single workout, is what to encourage.
- Plyometrics alone help most at easy and moderate paces (12 km/h or slower). That suits recreational runners.

### Gaps
- No trial found that tested a **purely bodyweight** home programme on running economy in recreational runners.
- Full-text effect sizes for Llanos-Lagos 2024 were not retrieved.

## 2. Does strength training reduce injury? (general, Nordic, Copenhagen, calf/soleus, hip/knee, foot)

### Takeaway
Strength training is the best-supported exercise intervention for preventing injuries in sport. Stretching does not prevent injuries. Running-specific prevention trials are mixed: a self-directed programme in first-time marathoners did nothing, while foot-core training in recreational runners cut injuries substantially. Targeted programmes (Nordic for hamstrings, Copenhagen for groin) come from team sports and transfer by inference.

### Cited Findings
- [Published] Lauersen, Bertelsen & Andersen 2014 (BJSM 48:871; 25 RCTs). Strength training reduced sports injuries to less than a third, and overuse injuries were almost halved. Proprioception and combined programmes also helped. **Stretching showed no significant effect** — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/48/11/871), [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/39522)
- [Published] Lauersen, Andersen & Andersen 2018 (BJSM 52:1557; 7,738 participants aged 12-40). Strength-training interventions gave a pooled injury RR of 0.338 (0.238-0.480). Meta-regression found that higher strength-training volume and intensity were associated with lower injury risk. A secondary listing reports that each **10% increase in volume cut risk by more than 4 percentage points** (not confirmed in the full text) — [PubMed 30131332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30131332/), [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/24/1557.info)
- [Published, runner-specific, negative] Toresdahl et al. 2020 (Sports Health) randomised first-time NYC marathoners to a self-directed strength programme or observation, starting 12 weeks before the race. Overuse injuries were **7.1% versus 7.3%**, with no difference in finish time — [PubMed 31642726](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31642726/), [PMC6931177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6931177)
- [Published, runner-specific, positive] Taddei et al. 2020 (Am J Sports Med; single-blind RCT; recreational long-distance runners followed for 1 year). The foot-core strengthening group had **14.0% injured versus 32.8%** in the control group, a 2.42-fold lower rate. The control did placebo static stretching. Time to injury correlated with gains in foot strength. Injuries were self-reported — [Physio-network](https://www.physio-network.com/?p=31789), [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/62997)
- [Published] Nordic hamstring exercise: van Dyk, Behan & Whiteley 2019 (BJSM; 15 studies; 8,459 athletes, mostly team sport). Pooled hamstring injury RR was 0.49 (0.32-0.74), and 0.52 (0.32-0.85) in RCTs only — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/21/1362). A later methodological reappraisal judged the preventive effect **inconclusive**, partly because some included trials combined the Nordic with other exercises (seen via a secondary summary) — [Physio-network](https://physio-network.com/research-reviews/thigh/why-methods-matter-in-a-meta-analysis-a-reappraisal-showed-inconclusive-injury-preventive-effect-of-nordic-hamstring-exercise)
- [Published] Copenhagen adductor programme: Harøy et al. 2019 (BJSM 53:150; cluster RCT; 652 Norwegian male footballers). One exercise with 3 progression levels: side-lying hip adduction, then short-lever Copenhagen, then long-lever Copenhagen. Dose was **3x/week in preseason (6-8 weeks), then 1x/week in season (28 weeks)**. Groin-problem prevalence was 13.5% versus 21.3% (OR 0.59, i.e. 41% lower risk). Players with pain above a threshold dropped to the lowest level. PEDro score 6/10, no blinding, self-reported outcomes — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/3/150.abstract), [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/55148)
- [Published] Patellofemoral pain: Nascimento et al. 2018 (14 trials, 673 participants). **Combined hip and knee strengthening** reduced pain and improved activity more than knee strengthening alone, without measurable strength change — [wikiMSK PDF](https://wikiMSK.org/w/img_auth.php/3/34/Hip_and_Knee_Strengthening_in_Patellofemoral_Syndrome_Metaanalysis_-_Nascimento_2018.pdf). Thomson et al. 2016 found hip and knee programmes about equally effective, with limited evidence for adding hip work — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/37501). A 2016 Gait & Posture review in runners found both gait retraining and proximal strengthening improved pain and function, but only gait retraining reduced peak hip adduction — [PubMed 26979886](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26979886/)
- [Published, unverified] A 2025 Clin J Sport Med paper reportedly linked a lower **single-leg glute bridge** self-test to overuse injury in NYC Marathon runners. Seen only via a Substack summary — [Substack](https://vitortessutti.substack.com/p/forca-ou-flexibilidade-na-prevencao)
- Soleus load:
  - [Secondary] Dorn, Schache & Pandy 2012 (J Exp Biol) modelled muscle forces in running and found the soleus highly active at all speeds — [PMC3979434](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3979434/)
  - [Coach/secondary] The "6-8x bodyweight" figure (sometimes about 8x for soleus versus about 3x for gastrocnemius) is widely repeated, but I could not confirm it in the primary text. Sources disagree (6.5-8x, up to 8-12x) — [LetsRun](https://www.letsrun.com/forum/flat_read.php?thread=13354703), [Foundation Clinic](https://www.foundationclinic.co.nz/blog/soleus-muscle-injuries/)
- [Published] Calf endurance norms: the single-leg heel-rise to fatigue (Hébert-Losier et al. 2017, 566 adults aged 20-81, 10-degree incline) had a median of 24 for men and 21 for women — [Waikato](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/items/c242f3d2-0b0a-4976-abc6-a7f7f9b13874/full). A 2025 Calf Raise App study (n = 500) found a median of about 25 per leg — [Leicester figshare](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/journal_contribution/Normative_values_for_calf_muscle_strength-endurance_in_the_general_population_assessed_with_the_Calf_Raise_Application_A_large_international_cross-sectional_study/30209584)

### Inferences
- The Toresdahl null result suggests that a short, self-directed, low-adherence programme started late adds little. Volume, intensity and duration matter (Lauersen 2018 dose-response). An app's adherence features and progression logic are probably as important as which exercises it picks.
- Foot-core work has the strongest *runner-specific* injury RCT of anything here. Yet the current A/B/C plan has none.
- A single-leg heel-raise test (target of 25 or more per leg) and a single-leg bridge test are cheap, home-friendly benchmarks an app could track.
- Nordic and Copenhagen evidence comes from football players. In runners it is plausible but not shown. A low dose (Copenhagen once a week in maintenance) was enough in-season in Harøy.

### Gaps
- No RCT found on hip abductor strengthening to *prevent* ITBS in runners. Evidence is treatment-oriented (PFP), and no ITBS-specific trial was retrieved.
- Full Nordic reappraisal numbers were not verified. It is commonly attributed to Impellizzeri et al. 2021, but I did not confirm the authorship.
- No primary verification of soleus force multiples.

## 3. Can bodyweight / high-rep / single-leg work substitute for heavy load?

### Takeaway
Partly. For **muscle size and endurance-strength**, low loads taken close to failure match heavy loads. For **maximal strength (1RM)**, heavy loads win. Maximal strength is the main route to RE gains, and in Llanos-Lagos submaximal-load training showed no RE effect. Without weights, the practical fix is to make each set *heavy relative to the person*. Options:
- single-leg variations
- a loaded backpack
- slow eccentrics or tempo
- pauses
- low rep ranges (around 5-8) at the hardest variation the person can manage
- plyometrics for the reactive-strength part

### Cited Findings
- [Published] Schoenfeld et al. 2017 (J Strength Cond Res; 21 studies; all sets to momentary failure, 6 weeks or longer). **1RM strength gains were significantly greater with high loads.** Hypertrophy was similar across loading ranges, and isometric strength did not differ — [PubMed 28834797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)
- [Published] A 2020 meta-analysis found no significant difference in type I or type II fibre hypertrophy between low- and high-load training, but with very wide intervals — [PubMed 33312275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33312275/)
- [Published] Llanos-Lagos 2024 found submaximal-load and isometric strength training did not significantly improve RE, while high-load and combined methods did (see Q1) — [UNAB](https://repositorio.unab.cl/items/13e3ac75-9905-4902-81f7-232752e84ae6/full)

### Inferences
- High-rep bodyweight squats (for example 3x25) are "submaximal load" and probably will not deliver the RE benefit. **The app should cap reps and push variation difficulty**: move to a harder variation once the top of a moderate rep range is reached. Hold sets near failure (about 1-3 reps in reserve) rather than letting reps climb.
- Hard, no-equipment lower-body ladders that approach "heavy":
  - squat → split squat → rear-foot-elevated (Bulgarian) split squat on the bed or chair → backpack-loaded RFESS → shrimp squat / pistol to box
  - glute bridge → single-leg bridge → single-leg hip thrust with shoulders on the bed → backpack-loaded
  - calf raise → single-leg off a step → single-leg with backpack → slow 3-second lowering
  - hamstring: Nordic lowering, as already in the plan
- A backpack of books (around 5-15 kg) is the cheapest way to add external load. It is the most important "equipment" upgrade for runner-relevant force levels.

### Gaps
- No direct study found comparing loaded-backpack or single-leg bodyweight programmes with barbell training on RE.

## 4. Plyometrics for runners: which, how much, how to progress

### Takeaway
Use low-amplitude, high-frequency reactive drills first (pogo hops, skipping, ankle hops), then progress to single-leg hops, bounds and, later, low drop jumps. The only trial-derived dosing found is the moderators above: 2 or more sessions a week for more than 7 weeks and more than 15 sessions. No primary source gave foot-contact numbers. Coach guidance converges on a beginner start of about 40-120 contacts per session, at most 2 sessions a week.

### Cited Findings
- [Published] More than 15 sessions, more than 7 weeks and more than 2 sessions a week were associated with larger RE effects — [Dudagoitia Barrio 2023](https://repositorio.unab.cl/items/2cf806a8-b611-4964-8dcb-f365dfa88ccc/full); 10 weeks or more — [Eihara 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9653533/)
- [Coach/secondary] Outside Online:
  - Beginners start at about 80-120 foot contacts per session, as 2-3 sets of 10-20 jumps.
  - Move to alternating single-leg landings after about 2 weeks of comfortable two-foot jumps.
  - Track foot contacts per session.
  - About 2 plyometric sessions a week is "probably the maximum for any distance runner."
  - Source: [Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/running/training/workouts/ready-for-power-building-plyometrics-take-this-test-and-start-with-these-exercises/?scope=anon)
- [Coach/secondary] Other guides disagree. One suggests 40-60 contacts per session for beginners (at most 2 sessions a week); another cites Central Performance at 60-120 contacts, 2-4 sessions a week. For pogos, land on the balls of the feet with a slight knee bend, 2-4 sets of 6 — [Pliability](https://pliability.com/stories/best-plyometrics-for-runners), [iRunFar](https://www.irunfar.com/three-plyometric-exercises-for-endurance-runners)

### Inferences
- Proposed app progression (coach-consensus, not trial-validated):
  1. Weeks 1-2: double-leg pogo hops, A-skips, skipping in place; about 40-60 contacts.
  2. Weeks 3-5: pogos with forward and lateral travel, alternating single-leg hops; about 60-100 contacts.
  3. Weeks 6 and on: single-leg pogos, bounds, and low box/step drop-to-rebound from a stair (about 20-30 cm); about 80-120 contacts.
  4. Throughout: 2 sessions a week, with full rest between sets and quality over fatigue.
- Gate the progression on prerequisites. Suggested gates:
  - about 20 or more single-leg heel raises per leg, and
  - pain-free hopping (no pain rising the next morning), and
  - optionally a 30-second single-leg balance.
- Plyometrics count as impact load. Treat them as part of running load: do them on a quality day or replace some strides, and avoid them during a mileage spike.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for foot-contact dosing in runners. Blagrove's practical papers likely have it but were not retrieved.
- No injury-rate data found for plyometric programmes in recreational runners.

## 5. Isometrics for tendon health (calf, mid-thigh, Spanish squat)

### Takeaway
Isometrics give short-term pain relief for patellar tendinopathy, and they are a reasonable tool for load management when tendons are irritable. They are not better than isotonic loading over the long term, and on their own they did not improve RE. Use them as an add-on or a rehab option, not as the core of the plan.

### Cited Findings
- [Published] Rio et al. 2015 (small, men only): isometric single-leg decline squat holds reduced patellar tendon pain immediately and for 45 minutes, more than isotonic exercise — [Physical Therapy First review](https://physicaltherapyfirst.com/?p=10327)
- [Published] A 2020 systematic review (BMJ Open SEM) found limited evidence that isometrics are **not superior** to isotonic exercise for chronic tendinopathy, either immediately or up to 12 weeks — [PMC7406028](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7406028/)
- [Published] Spanish squat: only biomechanical studies found. It loads the knee extensors and is suggested for patellar tendinopathy, but no clinical trial was found — [Brage](https://hvlopen.brage.unit.no/hvlopen-xmlui/handle/11250/3019124?show=full)
- [Published] Isometric strength training on its own did not significantly improve RE — [Llanos-Lagos 2024](https://repositorio.unab.cl/items/13e3ac75-9905-4902-81f7-232752e84ae6/full)

### Inferences
- App feature: a "sore tendon" or deload mode that swaps the dynamic calf and knee exercise for isometric holds. Typical clinical dosing is about 5 x 30-45 s at a hard but tolerable effort (coach/clinical convention, not verified here). Examples: wall-sit or Spanish squat with a towel around a door frame or table leg, and a single-leg calf raise hold at mid-range.
- Home Spanish squat setup: a strap or towel looped around a sturdy fixed object at shin height. This is a safety concern, so the app should give clear instructions.

### Gaps
- No long-term isometric-only tendon outcome data in runners.

## 6. Scheduling around runs: interference, frequency, maintenance

### Takeaway
2-3 sessions a week builds strength. In other team-sport/prevention contexts, about 1 session a week maintained protective benefits (Harøy in-season). Interference is small for recreational runners if strength work is kept away from key runs: on the same day as a hard run (run first, a gap of hours) or on easy days, and not the day before a long or quality run.

### Cited Findings
- [Published] 2-3 sessions a week is recommended for performance benefit — [Blagrove 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5889786/)
- [Published] Strength training 3x/week built capacity and 1x/week maintained it (in-season groin prevention) — [Harøy 2019](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/3/150.abstract)
- [Published/secondary] Interference is real but not consistent. In a 9-week study of active men, same-day order affected some power indices but not strength, lean mass or aerobic fitness, and order "could be dictated by personal preference" — [VU repository](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/40029/), [PMC7224562](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7224562)
- [Coach/secondary] For same-day strength and hard endurance: do the endurance session first, then wait at least 3 hours. Separating by 6 hours or more, or onto different days, largely removes interference. Interference grows with excessive endurance volume — [TrainingPeaks](https://trainingpeaks.com/blog/risks-of-concurrent-training/), [Foundation Clinic](https://www.foundationclinic.co.nz/blog/risks-of-concurrent-training/)

### Inferences (rules an app could encode)
- **Hard-day-hard / easy-day-easy:** put strength on the same day as intervals or tempo (after the run, ideally 3 hours or more later) or on an easy-run day. Avoid it the day before a long run or race, and avoid leg-heavy work within about 48 hours of a race.
- **Base phase:** 2-3 sessions a week with progression. **Build/peak phase:** 2 a week, holding difficulty steady and reducing sets. **Taper/race week:** 1 short session or none. **Off-season:** 3 a week is fine.
- Never let strength work fall below 1 session a week for more than about 2 weeks without a "restart" step-down.
- Plyometrics are best on the hard-run day or before the strength session, while fresh.

### Gaps
- Blagrove's specific periodisation guidance was not retrieved.
- No runner-specific trial on strength-session timing relative to long runs.

## 7. Established bodyweight programs and no-bar progressions

### Takeaway
The r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine (RR) is the de facto community standard. It uses paired exercises, 3 sets, and progression to a harder variation once a rep target is reached, with rows progressing vertical → inverted → wide. It explicitly offers **bedsheet-in-door rows and table rows** for people without a bar. The current plan's structure (3 sets, double progression up variation ladders) already matches this model.

### Cited Findings
- [Coach/community] RR row ladder: vertical row → inverted row → wide row, with "3x8 wide rows" as the point to move on. No-bar options are **incline rows with a bedsheet and a closed door**, or a table — [BWF wiki mirror](https://redlib.hackliberty.org/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/exercises/row), [mirror 2](https://asdjflk.github.io/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/exercises/row), [Antranik on minimal-equipment rows](https://antranik.org/rows/)
- [Coach/community] RR summaries and interactive versions — [luke-snaw notes](https://luke-snaw.github.io/notes/smvvuk92pf7i5f6hmviazba), [gist cheat sheet](https://gist.github.com/fi0/d13d851347c8e29e1a3e0ec7042fe149)

### Inferences
- From memory of the RR (not verified this session, because Reddit blocked fetching): 3 sets of 5-8 reps, progressing when 3x8 is reached; warm-up first; 3x/week. Treat these as unverified.
- No-bar pull ladder for the app, easiest to hardest:
  1. towel/sheet door row, standing more upright
  2. towel door row, feet closer to the door
  3. table row with knees bent (lying under a sturdy table, gripping the edge)
  4. table row with straight legs
  5. table row with feet elevated on a chair
  6. single-arm towel door row
  7. backpack-loaded table row
  - Also: backpack bent-over rows (already in the plan) and prone "Y/T/W" raises on the floor for the upper back.
- Convict Conditioning, GMB and Overcoming Gravity were not retrieved this session, so there are no cited specifics. Overcoming Gravity's core idea, progressing by leverage and variation within a strength rep range rather than adding endless reps, matches the Q3 inference.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the official RR wiki (Reddit blocks the fetcher).
- No sources retrieved for Convict Conditioning, GMB, Overcoming Gravity, Jay Dicharry, Tom Goom or Running Physio programmes.

## 8. Warm-up / mobility and foot strengthening

### Takeaway
Foot-core training has a positive runner RCT (Taddei 2020) and should be added. Stretching does not prevent injury (Lauersen 2014), so warm-ups should be dynamic and progressive rather than static stretching.

### Cited Findings
- [Published] The foot-core programme cut injuries to 14.0% versus 32.8% over 1 year in recreational runners — [Physio-network](https://www.physio-network.com/?p=31789)
- [Published] Stretching programmes did not significantly prevent sports injuries — [Lauersen 2014](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/48/11/871)

### Inferences
- Add a 5-8 minute "foot core" mini-block, at least 3x/week, possibly attached to runs. Content: short-foot holds, toe yoga (big toe up and down), towel scrunches, single-leg balance, and barefoot calf raises. These are typical components of such programmes; the exact Taddei protocol was not retrieved.
- Pre-run dynamic warm-up: leg swings, walking lunges, A-skips, and then pogos. This doubles as low-dose plyometric exposure.

### Gaps
- Exact Taddei protocol (exercises, frequency, progression) not retrieved.
- FIFA 11+ style evidence specific to runners was not searched.

## 9. Concrete suggested changes to the app's A/B/C plan

### Takeaway
The plan's structure (full-body, 3 sets, double progression up variation ladders, 2-3x/week) is consistent with Blagrove's 2-3 sessions a week and with RR practice. Its main weaknesses:
- lower-body ladders likely top out at "submaximal load" (little RE benefit);
- **no plyometrics** (missing the "combined" method that had the largest RE effect);
- no foot-core work (the best runner-specific injury RCT);
- an upper-body emphasis that is more than runners need;
- hip abduction is only indirect.

### Cited Findings
- Combined heavy + plyometric training had the largest RE effect; submaximal-load training showed none — [Llanos-Lagos 2024](https://repositorio.unab.cl/items/13e3ac75-9905-4902-81f7-232752e84ae6/full)
- Foot-core RCT in runners — [Taddei 2020](https://www.physio-network.com/?p=31789)
- Combined hip and knee strengthening for PFP — [Nascimento 2018](https://wikiMSK.org/w/img_auth.php/3/34/Hip_and_Knee_Strengthening_in_Patellofemoral_Syndrome_Metaanalysis_-_Nascimento_2018.pdf)
- Copenhagen 3-level ladder and 3x/1x dosing — [Harøy 2019](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/3/150.abstract)
- Nordic programmes cut hamstring injury by about half in team sports (contested) — [van Dyk 2019](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/21/1362)

### Inferences (recommended changes, with reasons)
1. **Add a short plyometric block (5-8 min) at the start of two of the three workouts**, or make it a separate "plyo" item on a hard-run day. Follow the Q4 progression and track foot contacts. *Reason:* the combined method had the largest RE effect, and plyometrics help RE at recreational speeds.
2. **Make every lower-body ladder reach single-leg plus backpack load, and cap reps** (for example progress at 3x8-12 per side, not 3x20+). Ladders:
   - squats: squat → split squat → RFESS on the bed → backpack RFESS → skater/shrimp squat
   - lunges: reverse lunge → deficit reverse lunge from a step → backpack
   - step-ups: higher step → backpack → slow 3-second lowering
   - calf raises: progress at about 3x15-20 single-leg, then add the backpack and a 3-second eccentric
   - *Reason:* 1RM-type strength needs heavy relative load, and high-rep submaximal work showed no RE gain.
3. **Split calf work into bent-knee (soleus) and straight-knee (gastrocnemius) raises.** Add a seated or bent-knee single-leg calf raise (knee bent about 30-45 degrees, or seated with the backpack on the knee). *Reason:* the soleus is the dominant force producer in running (Dorn 2012); straight-knee raises bias the gastrocnemius. Add a monthly **single-leg heel-raise test** with a target of about 25 or more per leg (norms of about 21-25).
4. **Add direct hip abductor work.** Side-lying hip abduction → side plank with top-leg raise → single-leg Romanian deadlift reach / hip hike off a step. It can be paired with the existing side plank in B. *Reason:* hip plus knee strengthening beats knee-only for PFP, a common runner complaint.
5. **Add foot-core work.** Short-foot holds, toe yoga and single-leg balance as a 5-minute finisher, at least 3x/week (it can live outside the A/B/C rotation, for example after easy runs). *Reason:* Taddei 2020.
6. **Copenhagen in C:** use Harøy's 3-level ladder (side-lying adduction → short-lever with knee on the bed or chair → long-lever) and its pain rule (step down a level if pain exceeds a threshold). Once in race build/peak, 1 set a week is enough.
7. **Nordic lowering in B is good; keep it, but dose it conservatively.** Use low reps (for example 2-3 sets of 3-6 slow lowers) and progress via range or lowering slowness. Avoid it within 48 hours before quality or long runs, because it causes significant soreness when new. Evidence is from team sports and contested, but the exercise is cheap.
8. **Trim the upper body.** A, B and C currently include push-ups, pike push-ups, two dips and two to three rows. For runners, cut to one push and one pull per session. Drop one of the dip slots (bed dips stress the anterior shoulder and add little for running) in favour of the hip abductor or soleus slot. Keep rows for posture and upper back balance.
9. **Use the no-bar row ladder** from Q7: towel door row → table row bent knees → straight → feet elevated → single-arm towel → backpack-loaded.
10. **Add schedule rules** (Q6): strength on hard-run days (after the run) or easy days, never the day before a long run or race; 2-3x/week in base, 2x in build, 1x in taper.
11. **Add tendon "irritable" mode** (Q5): swap dynamic knee and calf moves for isometric holds (wall-sit or Spanish squat, mid-range single-leg calf hold) when the user reports tendon pain.
12. **Prioritise adherence.** Use streaks, short-session options (a 15-20 minute minimum effective dose) and a programme of 10 weeks or more. *Reason:* Toresdahl's self-directed short programme failed; the dose-response (Lauersen 2018) and duration moderators (more than 7-10 weeks) favour sustained volume.

### Gaps
- None of these specific swaps has been trialled as a package. They are evidence-informed inferences.
- Exact rep thresholds for moving up each ladder are coach convention (RR-style), not trial-derived.
