# Competitor features of running and strength apps (2025-2026), with a gap analysis for a personal running + bodyweight strength app

Scope note: I used about 15 search and fetch calls. Coverage is strong for Runna, Garmin Coach/DSW, COROS EvoLab, Strava (Athlete Intelligence, subscription split, upload API), Stryd and Hevy/Strong. It is thin for Polar, Suunto, adidas Running, Apple Workout, TrainingPeaks, Fitbod, Caliber, GMB, Madbarz and OpenTracks. Those gaps are listed per section. Many search results were affiliate or competitor blogs. They are flagged where they are used.

## Q1. Which features are most valued, and which disliked or ignored, in each app?

### Takeaway
The features users mention most are shoe mileage, basic logging, social and kudos, guided audio runs (NRC) and adaptive pace targets (Runna). Segments and route planning split users: competitive and repeat-route runners love them, and others ignore them. Complaints focus on paywalls, "AI" insights that add little, chatty or out-of-sync audio coaching, and injuries from aggressive plans.

### Cited Findings
**Strava**
- Free users can create segments, view segment details and use Segment Explore, and they get segment PR notifications. Segment leaderboards and Route Builder have been subscription-only since the 2020 "Summit" overhaul. — [Triathlete](https://www.triathlete.com/gear/tech-wearables/strava-moves-segment-leaderboards-route-builder-to-subscription/?scope=anon)
- Beacon (live location sharing for safety) is now free for all users. — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/news/one-of-stravas-most-important-tools-is-now-free-for-everyone)
- US pricing is about $11.99/month or $79.99/year. A Strava + Runna bundle costs about $149.99/year. — [thepricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-strava-cost/); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- Athlete Intelligence left beta in early 2025. It gives post-upload insights based on the past 30 days of data. Wareable judged it "not currently adding much". — [Stuff](https://stuff.co.za/2025/02/21/stravas-athlete-intelligence-out-of-beta/); [Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/sport/is-strava-premium-worth-it)
- Fitness and Freshness is paywalled. One reviewer says the fatigue view is available only on desktop web, despite a decade of user requests for it in the apps. — [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/feature/strava-worth-paying-315565)
- Runners report using segments competitively ("super addicting") or as fitness markers on repeated routes. Others "never really understood segments" and use Strava only for mileage and kudos. Heatmaps and route planning are also valued, with route planning called "way better than Garmin's". — [Reddit/forum aggregation via search](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/Garmin/comments/1aupppi/stravadoes_it_really_add_anything_for_you); [HMRRC survey of club runners](https://hmrrc.com/members/pacesetter/2023/march/what-running-apps-our-runners-use-and-why)

**Shoe tracking (cross-app)**
- Shoe mileage is a recurring loved feature. Users say it is easier in Strava than in Garmin, and they praise apps that alert when a shoe hits the manufacturer's distance limit. Dedicated apps exist for it, such as ShoeCycle (miles plus purchase date) and TreadLeft (auto-import). — [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-web/46815/track-shoes); [ShoeCycle on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.shoecycle); [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1skn30p/snapshots/2026-04-14T01%3A16%3A20.115877Z)

**Nike Run Club**
- Audio Guided Runs launched in 2017 (NRC 5.9) with coaches and athletes, featuring guidance from Mo Farah and Kevin Hart. Later updates let you continue running after a guided run ends, keeping one continuous activity, and added Guided Run search. — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Nike-Run-Club-big-new-update-brings-audio-guided-runs-exclusive-Apple-Watch-features-and-more_id98544); [APKMirror release notes](https://apkmirror.com/apk/nike-inc/nike-run-club/nike-run-club-3-1-0-release)
- Reviews praise the guided runs. Criticisms include glitches, guided-run audio drifting out of sync with time and distance, and coaching that is "too chatty". — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/nike-run-club-review); [Kimola Play review analysis](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-nike-run-club-app-review-analysis-google-play-en-gb-154572)

**Runna**
- Users get a Pace Status after each paced workout ("Pace on Point", "Ahead of the Pack"). Recommended pace changes need the user's approval and can be reverted. — [Runna support](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/14656203-what-are-pace-insights-and-how-do-they-work)
- The Wall Street Journal reported physical therapists seeing "multiple Runna-related injury cases each week". Runna has since added ways to reduce plan intensity. The algorithm is not published. Users cannot set a goal time directly: they raise their estimated race time instead, which makes every pace harder. — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- Pricing is $119.99/year standalone. Strava acquired Runna in April 2025. — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)

**Garmin**
- Garmin Coach plans (5K, 10K, half marathon) are free. Users pick a coach: Greg McMillan, Amy Parkerson-Mitchell, or Jeff Galloway (run-walk-run). A separate "Garmin Run Coach" balances training and recovery days and can add strength sessions on supported watches. — [Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-GB/blog/garmin-training-plans-for-runners/); [Garmin DE](https://www.garmin.com/de-DE/blog/?p=4420)
- A blog review says DSW lacks periodization and race-specific preparation. — [gneta.app (blog, unverified)](https://www.gneta.app/blog/garmin-suggested-workouts-guide)

**Strength loggers (Hevy, Strong)**
- Hevy's free tier allows about 4 routines, 7 custom exercises and 3 months of history, with ads. Strong's free tier allows 3 or 4 custom routines (sources disagree). Both auto-detect PRs with an in-workout celebration. Hevy has a social feed and a web app. Strong has share links only. Both export CSV. Pro costs about $23.99/year (Hevy; lifetime $74.99) and about $29.99/year (Strong). — [lastlift comparison (competitor blog)](https://lastlift.pages.dev/articles/hevy-vs-strong/); [sensai pricing (competitor blog)](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-pricing-free-tier-comparison)

**Bodyweight apps**
- The r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine is most often followed with dedicated loggers: "Bodyweight Fitness" (iOS) and Fitloop (4.59/5 from 70 US ratings). Fitloop is praised for built-in instruction videos and free logging. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1018863605); [Apptail Fitloop](https://apptail.io/app/fitloop-bodyweight-fitness-YCs/united-states)
- Freeletics is a bodyweight-only app with an "AI Coach" that generates personalized workouts. It has a free version, and paid plans start around $1.38/week (affiliate source). — [Freeletics](https://www.freeletics.com/en/bodyweight-training/); [hotelgyms (affiliate)](https://www.hotelgyms.com/blog/the-freeletics-fitness-app-review)

**Open source**
- FitoTrack is a GPLv3, ad-free, no-tracking Android tracker for running, cycling and hiking with charts and stats (F-Droid, v16.3, Android 8+). OpenTracks is listed as the top free, open-source Strava alternative. — [F-Droid FitoTrack](https://f-droid.org/ko/packages/de.tadris.fitness/); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/strava/?tag=fitness-tracker)

### Inferences
- For a single user, the "loved" features that do not need a social graph are shoe mileage, PR celebrations, guided audio and adaptive pace targets. Of these, only shoe mileage is missing from the app, which already has audio coaching, Tune-In and PBs.
- Users repeatedly reject opaque "AI insight" text (Strava Athlete Intelligence) and over-aggressive plans (Runna injury reports). The app's existing RPE-drift and long-run jump check in the Monday report is in the "explainable" style that users trust more.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve direct Reddit r/running or r/AdvancedRunning threads (search returned mirrors and blogs). Survey-level evidence, such as percentages of users who use a given feature, was not found.
- No current sources were found for adidas Running, Apple Workout/Fitness (custom workouts, race route), TrainingPeaks (ATL/CTL/TSB PMC chart, structured workout builder), Fitbod/Caliber, GMB, Madbarz, or OpenTracks feature lists. Strava's current free-versus-paid status for shoe tracking and the global heatmap was not confirmed.

## Q2. How do the flagship "smart" features actually work?

### Takeaway
Most "adaptive" features are simple, explainable rules. COROS uses a 42-day versus 7-day load ratio. Runna adjusts pace only when it sees a consistent pattern across speed sessions, and only with user approval. Garmin DSW gates on having a VO2max and training status and re-plans daily from recovery and load. None of this needs server-side ML. It can be reproduced on-device from per-run load numbers like the app's existing session-RPE load.

### Cited Findings
**Runna adaptive plans / Pace Insights**
- Plan inputs are current ability, experience, weekly running days, race distance and date, and chosen volume and difficulty (changeable any time). One estimated race time drives every pace target across all distances. — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- Pace Insights is the sole adaptive mechanism. After each workout with pace targets (intervals, tempo, time trials, some long runs) the user gets a Pace Status. If a consistent pattern appears across eligible sessions, Runna suggests speeding up or slowing down. Accepting changes only the pace targets, not the plan structure. It never changes paces automatically, and changes can be reverted from the recommendation history. Free runs and manual uploads are not analysed. It is not available on New to Running, Return to Running or parkrun plans. — [Runna support](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/14656203-what-are-pace-insights-and-how-do-they-work)
- It "ignores reps that look wrong", and "a single fast session is not enough". "Not Feeling 100%" adapts training for illness, aches or lack of time and returns the runner to the plan "in a structured way". Plans include strength, warm-up, stretch and stability sessions. — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)
- Adapt for Heat (July 2026) adjusts pace targets for local heat and humidity using forecast data, not measured heat strain. — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/07/29/runna-launches-adapt-for-heat-to-adjust-pace-for-weather/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/29/runna-now-automatically-adapts-training-paces-based-on-heat-and-humidity/)
- Race predictions assume good conditions on a flat road. "Hills, heat and wind are not part of the predicted range." — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/)

**Garmin Daily Suggested Workouts**
- DSW needs a training status and a VO2max estimate first. Suggestions are built from past activity and update as recovery time, training habits and VO2max change. For cycling, it needs a week of riding with HR and power. — [Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-D3621248-CC5B-4F04-B004-6F6FD9FDFEB0/EN-US/GUID-AFECC098-0B34-4913-9944-66E8DAF64039.html); [Garmin Edge manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals-apac/webhelp/edge850/EN-SG/GUID-6F797AF0-431C-4056-BA97-3A8A1F07620A-219.html)
- Training Readiness is a 0-100 score built from recovery time, sleep, HRV status, stress and acute load. When readiness is low, DSW trends toward easy runs or rest. These are secondary sources, not Garmin documentation. — [the5krunner Garmin explainer](https://the5krunner.com/garmin-features/training/); [gneta.app](https://www.gneta.app/blog/garmin-suggested-workouts-guide)
- Garmin's race-adaptive plans factor terrain, direction and wind speed into race-time prediction and adapt to performance and recovery. — [Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-GB/blog/garmin-training-plans-for-runners/)

**COROS EvoLab**
- Base Fitness is a 42-day rolling average of daily training load. Load Impact is the last 7 days. Intensity Trend is Load Impact divided by Base Fitness: above 100% means building, below 100% means declining. The status band thresholds differ between COROS pages and versions (e.g. "Optimized" at 100-150% in one article). — [COROS support](https://support.coros.com/hc/en-us/articles/360061452651); [COROS Training Status](https://au.coros.com/stories/d/prepare-for-your-next-race-with-training-status); [COROS productive load](https://www.coros.com/stories/coros-coaches/c/what-is-a-productive-training-load)
- EvoLab also includes recovery metrics and a Training Load story/explainer. — [COROS EvoLab](https://coros.com/evolab); [COROS recovery metrics](https://coros.com/stories/coros-metrics/c/your-coros-recovery-metrics-explained)

**Strava Athlete Intelligence / Fitness**
- Athlete Intelligence produces generated insights right after upload, using the past 30 days. It is subscriber-only and was out of beta by February 2025. — [Stuff](https://stuff.co.za/2025/02/21/stravas-athlete-intelligence-out-of-beta/); [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/stravas-athlete-intelligence-translates-workout-data-into-simple-and-personalized-insights-302266680.html)
- Fitness Score is part of the paid Fitness and Freshness suite of training load and effect metrics. — [Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/sport/is-strava-premium-worth-it)

**Stryd**
- Critical Power is auto-estimated from 3-5 runs. Since February 2025 it can give a single-run initial estimate. It can also be set manually from a recent 5K or 10K, or from a 6-3 Laps or 9-3 Minute test done within 30 days. The race power calculator predicts race performance from recent training, and Stryd warns its accuracy depends on the CP estimate. — [Stryd help](https://help.stryd.com/en/articles/8258035-estimated-critical-power); [Stryd blog 2025](https://blog.stryd.com/2025/02/20/new-feature-update-start-power-based-training-with-stryd-after-just-one-run/); [Stryd manual CP](https://help.stryd.com/en/articles/8718390-manually-enter-critical-power)

**NRC Audio Guided Runs**
- These are pre-recorded coach and athlete audio sessions, synced to time or distance, that can be followed by continued free running in one activity. — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Nike-Run-Club-big-new-update-brings-audio-guided-runs-exclusive-Apple-Watch-features-and-more_id98544); [APKMirror](https://apkmirror.com/apk/nike-inc/nike-run-club/nike-run-club-3-1-0-release)

**Polar / Suunto**
- Suunto forum users report that the Suunto app puts threshold at the Z4/Z5 boundary, and they ask for a Polar-style running test ("I miss Polar simplicity"). — [Suunto forum](https://forum.suunto.com/post/140456)

### Inferences
- The COROS ratio is essentially an ACWR (acute:chronic workload ratio) with 7/42-day windows. The app already computes session-RPE load, so a 7d/42d ratio with a "building / maintaining / detraining / overreaching" label is a small JS addition to the weekly report.
- A Runna-style "pace insight" could reuse Tune-In and interval-course results. Flag 3 or more consecutive sessions beating or missing target by more than X s/km, then propose (not auto-apply) new targets. The Runna rules of "ignore bad reps, one session isn't enough, user approves" are a good template.
- A DSW-like "today's suggestion" could combine the 7/42 ratio, last-run RPE, and Health Connect resting HR and sleep, which the app already reads, choosing between rest, easy, long and quality sessions.
- "Adapt for Heat" is feasible because the app already stores weather per run. Pre-run, fetch a forecast and adjust Tune-In target pace.

### Gaps
- Polar Running Program, Polar Fitness Test (resting-HR-based VO2max estimate), and Suunto's training features were not confirmed from 2025-2026 sources. Garmin's exact DSW algorithm (Firstbeat) is not public. Strava's exact Fitness formula (believed to be impulse-response, Banister style) was not confirmed in this research.

## Q3. Which of these features are feasible for a single developer (Android Capacitor + JS, Supabase only)?

### Takeaway
Nearly everything that does not need a social graph or a map tile/routing server is feasible client-side. Strava upload is notably easy: one OAuth app, one POST of a generated GPX/TCX/FIT file, then poll for status. It even supports a JSON format for strength workouts. Live tracking (Beacon-style) is feasible with Supabase realtime plus a share link. Segments, heatmaps and route building are the heaviest items.

### Cited Findings
- Strava uploads accept FIT, TCX and GPX (and .gz versions), plus JSON for WeightTraining, HighIntensityIntervalTraining, Workout and Crossfit. They need the `activity:write` scope. The upload is asynchronous: POST /api/v3/uploads, then poll GET /uploads/:id at most once per second (mean processing under 2 s) until `activity_id` appears. `external_id` is used to avoid duplicates. `sport_type` overrides detection. — [Strava API Uploads](https://developers.strava.com/docs/uploads/)
- Default Strava app limits are 200 requests per 15 minutes and 2,000 per day overall. Non-upload calls are capped at 100 per 15 minutes and 1,000 per day. Upload endpoints are excluded from the non-upload bucket. Apps need review to scale beyond a few connected athletes, which is irrelevant for a single user. — [Strava rate limits](https://developers.strava.com/docs/rate-limits/)
- Open-source Android trackers (FitoTrack, OpenTracks) show that a full tracker with stats and charts is maintainable as a hobby project. — [F-Droid FitoTrack](https://f-droid.org/ko/packages/de.tadris.fitness/)

### Inferences (feasibility rating for this app; my judgment, not sourced)
- **Easy (days):** GPX/TCX export from stored raw GPS plus HR/cadence; Strava upload (OAuth via a custom-scheme redirect, with the token in Supabase or local storage); shoe tracking with mileage alerts; manual lap button; auto-pause (speed threshold in the foreground service); a 7d/42d load ratio; race predictor for 10K/HM (Riegel or Daniels VDOT, already partly present); a training calendar view of past and planned sessions; a Runna-style pace-insight suggestion.
- **Moderate (1-2 weeks):** a goal-race multi-week plan generator (rules-based Daniels/Pfitzinger-style template scaled by current VDOT and available days, with an RPE-based "not feeling 100%" downshift); heat-adjusted pace targets from forecast; an Android home-screen widget (needs a native plugin); a Beacon-style live share via Supabase realtime plus a public read-only page; music control via MediaSession media-button intents.
- **Hard or poor fit:** segments (needs matching of GPS traces to defined polylines, which is feasible locally for your own routes, i.e. "personal segments", and overlaps with the existing "routes ranked by run" ghost feature); global heatmaps (no data); route builder (needs a routing engine such as an OSRM/GraphHopper public API, which is possible but adds dependencies); Wear OS app or tiles (separate Kotlin module, sizeable effort).

### Gaps
- I did not verify Health Connect write-back of exercise sessions (an alternative to Strava export) or Capacitor plugins for widgets or Wear OS.

## Q4. Small but high-delight features, and gap analysis against this app

### Takeaway
The best value-for-effort gaps are: (1) Strava upload and GPX export, (2) shoe mileage, (3) a goal-race plan with Runna-style approval-based pace updates, (4) a COROS-style 7/42 load ratio and a daily suggestion, (5) a lap button and auto-pause, and (6) safety live-share. For strength, the gaps versus Hevy, Strong, Freeletics and the RR loggers are auto-PR celebration on progression ladders, exercise demo media, and strength sessions folded into the run plan (as Runna and Garmin Run Coach do), all bodyweight-only to match the user's no-equipment setup.

### Cited Findings
- Shoe mileage alerts are a frequently loved, low-tech feature. — [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-web/46815/track-shoes); [ShoeCycle](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.shoecycle)
- Safety beacon is free on Strava, so Strava treats it as a baseline safety feature. — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/news/one-of-stravas-most-important-tools-is-now-free-for-everyone)
- Runna and Garmin Run Coach both add strength sessions into running plans. — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/); [Garmin DE](https://www.garmin.com/de-DE/blog/?p=4420)
- Hevy and Strong celebrate auto-detected PRs in-workout and export CSV for free. — [lastlift (competitor blog)](https://lastlift.pages.dev/articles/hevy-vs-strong/)
- Fitloop is valued for built-in exercise instruction videos when following the RR. — [Apptail](https://apptail.io/app/fitloop-bodyweight-fitness-YCs/united-states)
- NRC lets you keep running after a guided run in one continuous activity. — [APKMirror](https://apkmirror.com/apk/nike-inc/nike-run-club/nike-run-club-3-1-0-release)
- Garmin offers a run-walk-run coach (Galloway) for beginners and injury prevention. — [Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-GB/blog/garmin-training-plans-for-runners/)

### Gaps
