# Evidence-based running training methods for recreational runners, and how to encode them in an adaptive app

Scope note: this pass used about 18 search calls, mostly abstracts and secondary summaries. Some primary papers were not opened in full; where a figure comes only from a secondary source it is marked. "Published" means peer-reviewed. "Coach/vendor" means coach writing, books or company pages.

## Q1. Which methods have the strongest evidence for recreational runners (3-4 runs/week, 20-40 km/week)?

### Takeaway
No intensity-distribution model (polarized, pyramidal, threshold-heavy) clearly beats the others for recreational runners. What the evidence does support: mostly easy running plus 1-2 harder sessions a week, careful load progression (especially single-session spikes), and a disciplined taper. Polarized training shows a small, inconsistent edge on VO2peak and time-trial results, mostly in short (<12 week) studies.

### Cited Findings
**Intensity distribution (Published)**
- Muñoz et al. 2014 (IJSPP), recreational runners, 10 weeks, randomized: polarized (mostly below VT1) vs "between-thresholds" training. 10K improved 5.0% vs 3.6% (about 41 s apart), but the difference between groups was **not significant**. The authors said polarized "may" give greater gains. — [PubMed 23752040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23752040/)
- Festa et al. 2020 (Frontiers Sports Act Living), 38 recreational runners, 8 weeks, zones set by HR from a max test. Polarized 77/3/20 (Z1/Z2/Z3) vs "focused" 40/50/10. Both improved by similar amounts, and the focused model used **17% less training time**. — [Frontiers fspor.2019.00070](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2019.00070); [PMC7739641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739641)
- Rosenblat et al. 2019 meta-analysis: moderate effect favoring polarized over threshold training on time-trial performance (ES −0.66, 95% CI −1.17 to −0.15), but only 3 studies were pooled. — [PubMed 29863593](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29863593/)
- Silva Oliveira et al. systematic review and meta-analysis: polarized beat other models for VO2peak only, with no advantage on other performance measures. The advantage showed up mainly in interventions shorter than 12 weeks. — [PMC11329428](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11329428)
- Rivera-Köfler et al. 2025 scoping review (JSCR 39(3):373-385): polarized and pyramidal both improved speed/power at the aerobic threshold, and every model improved speed/power at the anaerobic threshold. Test methods varied widely between studies. — [lida.sport-iat.de record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4091700?lng=en)
- A 2025 network meta-analysis led by Rosenblat (with Seiler) reportedly found no clear winner and no difference in VO2max between models. **Secondary podcast summaries only; not verified against the paper.** — [Podbean summary](https://www.podbean.com/media/share/pb-kejxn-184411c?download=1); [Escape Collective](https://escapecollective.com/which-popular-training-model-works-best-yes/)
- Trials still running or unpublished: a 4-arm polarized vs pyramidal (± mindfulness) 12-week 10K RCT protocol in Retos ([Retos 106895](https://recyt.fecyt.es/index.php/retos/article/view/106895)); NCT07565168, polarized vs pyramidal vs threshold over 8 weeks in recreational runners, listed as completed with no results found ([Syfrah](https://www.syfrah.com/trial/NCT07565168)); NCT07498608 ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07498608)). An Esteve-Lanao RCT of polarized vs pyramidal in recreational marathoners exists, but I did not retrieve its findings ([UA observatorio](https://observatorio-cientifico.ua.es/documentos/6a81a772926b1d60e686fd55)).

**Load progression and injury (Published)**
- Frandsen et al. 2025 (BJSM), 18-month cohort of about 5,200 runners. Each session's distance was compared with the **longest run in the previous 30 days**. Compared with ≤10% (or less), hazard ratios were: >10-30% spike 1.64; >30-100% spike 1.52; >100% spike 2.28. **Week-to-week change in weekly distance was not associated with injury.** The acute:chronic workload ratio (ACWR) gave counter-intuitive "protective" results. The study is observational and exploratory, with self-reported overuse injury. — [BJSM blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/?p=11751); [LIH research portal](https://researchportal.lih.lu/en/publications/how-much-running-is-too-much-identifying-high-risk-running-sessio/); [Physiotutors summary](https://www.physiotutors.com/research/running-injury-prevention/)
- Nielsen et al. 2014 (JOSPT 44(10):739-747), 874 novice runners with GPS watches, 1 year, 202 injuries. Weekly progression groups (<10%, 10-30%, >30%) did not differ significantly overall. Runners who progressed >30% over 2 weeks appeared more prone to "distance-related" injuries (e.g. patellofemoral pain, ITB syndrome, shin splints). Advice: novices should progress **<30%/week over a 2-week period**. The 10% rule itself was not supported as a threshold. — [JOSPT patient page PDF](https://www.orthobethesda.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/jospt-running-increased-mileage.pdf); [AAU record](https://vbn.aau.dk/da/publications/excessive-progression-in-weekly-running-distance-and-risk-of-runn); [Groningen GPS study](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/can-gps-be-used-to-detect-deleterious-progression-in-training-vol/)

**Taper (Published)**
- Bosquet et al. 2007 meta-analysis (27 studies, mostly competitive athletes): best results came from a 2-week taper (ES 0.59) with volume cut **41-60%** (ES 0.72) and intensity and frequency kept. Performance was more sensitive to cutting volume than to other changes. — [PubMed 17762369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/)
- A later endurance-only meta-analysis (14 studies) found a taper of ≤21 days with volume cut progressively by 41-60%, intensity and frequency unchanged, to be effective. — [Sponet record](https://www.sponet.de/sponet/Record/4081751?lng=en)
- Smyth & Lawlor 2021 (Frontiers), Strava data on 158,117 recreational marathoners: a strict 3-week taper was associated with a median **5 min 32 s (2.6%)** faster finish than a minimal taper. Strict (steady week-on-week) reductions did better than relaxed ones (p<0.001). 64% of runners tapered ≤2 weeks or inconsistently. The data are observational and confounded by training volume. — [PMC8506252](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8506252); [8020 Endurance commentary](https://www.8020endurance.com/new-study-shows-how-to-taper-better-for-your-next-marathon/) (4 weeks gave no extra gain, per that commentary)

**Beginner programs and run-walk (Published)**
- Relph et al. 2023 (IJERPH), modified 9-week Couch to 5K, n=110: only **27.3% completed**, and 19% reported injuries. Previous injury raised the odds of a new injury (OR 7.56). Dropouts' interviews raised three themes: injury, negative emotions about not finishing, and program design. The authors call for injury-prevention advice and more flexible program designs, and note that C25K's design lacks empirical evidence. — [PMC10487403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10487403/)
- Hottenrott et al. 2016 (JSAMS), randomized, n=42 marathoners: run/walk finished in 4:14:25 vs 4:07:40 for running only. The difference was **not significant** (p=0.377), with less muscle pain and fatigue in the run/walk group and similar cardiac load. The trial did not measure injury incidence. Claims that Galloway's method cuts injuries come from his own program data. — [BL record](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100029893803_0x000001); [RunnersConnect](https://runnersconnect.net/run-walk-method/)

### Inferences
- For 3-4 runs/week at 20-40 km, the safest default is **pyramidal or 80/20-ish**: about 75-85% easy, 1 quality session per week (2 for experienced runners), plus a long run. Festa suggests a threshold-heavier mix may be just as good and more time-efficient for time-limited amateurs. A polarized vs pyramidal choice is low-stakes, so it can be a user preference.
- The best-evidenced injury guard is a **per-session cap relative to the longest run in the last 30 days**, not a weekly 10% rule. This directly fits the app's per-run data.
- Run-walk is a legitimate first-class mode for beginners and returning runners, not a fallback. C25K's ~73% non-completion argues for flexible, repeatable weeks rather than a fixed 9-week march.

### Gaps
- No RCT found on 3 vs 4 vs 5 runs/week in recreational runners. Weekly frequency guidance remains coach convention.
- Full texts of Frandsen 2025 and the 2025 Rosenblat network meta-analysis were not read, so subgroup results (novice vs experienced, sex) are unknown.
- Stöggl & Sperlich 2014 (Front Physiol, polarized vs HIIT/threshold/high-volume in well-trained athletes) was not retrieved in this pass, so its numbers are not cited here.

## Q2. What exact prescription rules could an app follow (paces, % volume, session types)?

### Takeaway
Use VDOT (from a recent race or the app's Riegel 5K prediction) to set five pace bands: Easy, Marathon, Threshold, Interval, Repetition. Threshold at about 88% of vVO2max is the most consistently quoted anchor. Keep about 80% of time easy. Grow the long run and single sessions only relative to the longest run in the last 30 days. Taper by cutting volume about 40-60% while keeping intensity.

### Cited Findings
- Commonly quoted Daniels percentages (secondary calculators; they vary and sometimes mix %VO2max with %vVO2max): Easy ~65-70% (some quote a 59-74% band), Marathon ~79-82%, **Threshold ~88%**, Interval ~95-100% (about 3K-5K race pace), Repetition is set by duration rather than a fixed % (some sites say ~105-110%). — [the5krunner VDOT calculator](https://the5krunner.com/tools/running-calculators/vdot-calculator/); [runcalcs](https://runcalcs.pages.dev/training/jack-daniels-vdot-calculator/); [rundida](https://rundida.com/tools/vdot-calculator/)
- Threshold repeats are typically 5-10 min at about 88% vVO2max (Daniels "cruise intervals"). — [fitmetriclab](https://fitmetriclab-com.pages.dev/en/tools/running/training-paces-vdot/)
- Festa et al. distributions, usable as two templates: polarized 77/3/20 and "focused" 40/50/10, both effective over 8 weeks. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2019.00070)
- Norwegian model (Casado et al. 2023 review, IJERPH): threshold work between LT1 and LT2, roughly 2.0-4.5 mmol/L lactate, inside high-volume low-intensity training. The authors say controlled studies are still needed. — [RunnersTribe](https://runnerstribe.com/features/norwegian-model-unveiling-the-effectiveness-in-endurance-training/); [Trail Runner](https://www.trailrunnermag.com/training/the-norwegian-method-for-endurance-training/?scope=anon)
- Injury guard: keep each session's distance ≤110% of the longest run in the previous 30 days (Frandsen 2025). — [BJSM blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/?p=11751)
- Novice weekly progression <30% over 2 weeks (Nielsen 2014). — [JOSPT PDF](https://www.orthobethesda.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/jospt-running-increased-mileage.pdf)
- Taper: 2-3 weeks, volume −41-60% in steady steps, intensity and frequency kept. — [PubMed 17762369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/); [PMC8506252](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8506252)
- Garmin/Firstbeat load-ratio band (acute vs chronic): 0.8-1.4 "optimal", <0.8 "low" (vendor heuristic, not validated for injury). — [Garmin Quatix manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals-apac/webhelp/quatix7series/EN-SG/GUID-38144876-E1D1-4961-AF15-D36AAB615970-6863.html)

### Inferences (proposed app rules)
- **Pace derivation:** VDOT from the best recent race or effort. Fall back to the Riegel 5K prediction, but only from runs in the last ~6 weeks, and weight hard efforts over easy ones. Bands: E = VDOT easy range; T = 88% vVO2max (about 1-hour race pace); I = about 3K-5K pace; R = about mile pace, used only for short reps of 200-400 m; strides by feel.
- **Weekly templates (3 runs):** 1 quality session (alternate T and I weeks) + 1 easy + 1 long run. **(4 runs):** 1-2 quality + 1-2 easy (one with 4-6 × 20 s strides) + long run. Long run ≈ 25-35% of weekly volume for 20-40 km/week, and capped by the 110%-of-30-day-longest rule.
- **Quality volume caps (Daniels convention, coach opinion):** T ≤ ~10% of weekly volume per session; I ≤ ~8% of weekly volume or 10 km, whichever is less; R ≤ ~5%. Treat these as coach heuristics, not verified in this pass.
- **Progression and deloads:** increase weekly volume by ≤10-20% in build weeks (more conservative than Nielsen's 30% threshold). Every 3rd or 4th week, cut volume ~20-30% (coach convention). Weekly change matters less than avoiding single-session spikes.
- **Prescription mode:** pace for T/I/R (the app already does pace-coached intervals). Easy runs as RPE ≤3-4/10, "conversational", or a HR cap, so heat, hills and fatigue don't push easy runs too hard. The app's RPE-at-pace drift signal is a natural check that easy runs are truly easy.

### Gaps
- I could not open Daniels' Running Formula, Pfitzinger's Advanced Marathoning or Hansons' book directly, so exact volume-cap percentages and phase lengths are not primary-sourced. Commonly reported structure (coach opinion, unverified here): Daniels uses 4 phases (Foundation/Injury prevention → Early Quality → Transition Quality → Final Quality), each up to ~6 weeks. Pfitzinger uses mesocycles with lactate-threshold and medium-long runs and a 3-week taper. Hansons caps the long run at ~16 mi and relies on "cumulative fatigue" with 6 days/week. Hansons' 6-day frequency is above the 3-4 runs/week target.
- No head-to-head trial found comparing HR-zone vs pace vs RPE prescription in recreational runners.
- No trial evidence found on time-based vs distance-based plans. Time-based plans have a logical advantage (they equalize stress across paces for slower runners), but that is inference.

## Q3. How should a plan adapt to missed runs, high RPE, illness, or predicted fitness changes?

### Takeaway
Commercial apps converge on a few rules: don't make up missed sessions, re-plan forward, adjust paces only from repeated over- or under-performance on quality sessions, offer a time-limited "unwell" mode, and adjust pace for heat. Illness guidance (neck check, fever means no running) is expert opinion, not outcome data.

### Cited Findings
- **TrainAsONE (vendor):** a missed workout is dropped, not rescheduled. The next session is adjusted. "Missed sessions are data, not debt." The plan is re-evaluated after every run, missed run, or preference change. — [TrainAsONE FAQ](https://trainasone.com/faqs/2025/i-missed-a-workout-and-now-it-has-disappeared-how-can-i-do-it-today); [How it works](https://trainasone.com/how-it-works)
- **Runna (vendor/third-party):** paces are anchored to an estimated race time. Targets change when the runner *repeatedly* misses or beats them on tempo and interval sessions, and outlier reps are ignored. Runs can be skipped or rearranged, and several missed runs in a row trigger a check-in and re-plan. "Not Feeling 100%" adjusts up to 14 days: lower difficulty, or keep only easy and long runs. "Adapt for Heat" (July 2026) slows targets based on forecast heat and humidity so the stimulus stays the same. — [the5krunner, Is Runna AI](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/28/is-runna-ai/); [Runna skip help](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/15012850-how-and-when-to-skip-a-run-managing-missed-sessions-in-your-training-plan); [Runna unwell help](https://intercom.help/runna/en/articles/12809806-feeling-unwell-how-to-adapt-your-training-plan); [9to5mac heat](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/29/runna-now-automatically-adapts-training-paces-based-on-heat-and-humidity/)
- **Garmin DSW / Firstbeat (vendor):** Training Status uses VO2max trend, acute load (EPOC-weighted recent load) and HRV status. DSWs reportedly use Training Readiness, Training Status and load focus (low aerobic / high aerobic / anaerobic). No independent validation of DSW was found. — [Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals-apac/webhelp/marqaviatorgen2/EN-SG/GUID-4F6086CD-261C-427B-AF6F-00D456929455-8209.html); [the5krunner DSW](https://the5krunner.com/garmin-features/training/daily-suggested-workouts/)
- **Illness (expert opinion):** "Neck check": symptoms only above the neck → easy running with less duration and intensity. Below the neck (chest, fever, aches, GI) → rest. Fever (>37.5 °C) is a hard stop because of myocarditis risk. A common approach is ≥24 h fever-free before any running and about a week before hard sessions. Restart at about 50% of usual time, easy, and build over several days. No authoritative outcome-based guideline was found. — [Runner's World AU](https://runnersworldonline.com.au/running-after-illness/); [Women's Running](https://www.womensrunning.com/training/recovery/runners-dealing-with-illness/?scope=anon); [Runlovers](https://runlovers.it/en/2026/running-after-the-flu-the-neck-rule-that-tells-you-when-its-safe-to-start-again/)
- Frandsen 2025 implies that after a layoff the 30-day longest run falls, so the safe single-session cap falls with it. — [BJSM blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/?p=11751)

### Inferences (proposed app rules)
- **Missed run:** never stack it. If it was a quality session, move it only when there are ≥48 h before the next quality session or long run; otherwise drop it. Missing ≥1 week → re-entry week at ~70-80% of the last full week. Missing ≥2 weeks → recompute the 30-day longest-run cap and rebuild from there. Missing ≥4 weeks → reduce VDOT ~1-2 points per week out (heuristic, not sourced) and re-test with a time trial.
- **RPE higher than expected:** the app already logs RPE and RPE-at-pace drift. If session RPE exceeds the target by ≥2 on two consecutive runs, or easy-pace RPE drifts upward over 7-10 days, swap the next quality session for easy running and freeze progression for a week. If it recurs, run a deload week. A single high-RPE run should only trigger a check (heat, sleep, illness), mirroring Runna's "repeatedly" rule.
- **Illness:** a "not feeling 100%" toggle with a neck-check prompt. Below-neck symptoms or fever → rest days, then ≥24 h fever-free → 2-3 short easy runs at ~50% duration → resume quality ~7 days after fever. Cap the long run by the recomputed 30-day rule.
- **Fitness change:** raise VDOT only when ≥2 quality sessions are beaten at target RPE, or a new race or time-trial prediction exceeds the current value. Raise ≤1 VDOT point per 3-4 weeks (heuristic) so paces don't jump. Lower VDOT after long layoffs or repeated misses. Heat-adjust pace targets from the weather data the app already collects.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of any adaptive app's (Runna, TrainAsONE, Garmin DSW, COROS, Humango) effect on outcomes or injury was found. Humango and COROS methods were not covered in this pass.
- The size of VDOT decay per week of detraining is not sourced here.

## Q4. What is the evidence on sub-threshold "Norwegian singles" for amateurs?

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed trial exists. The method is a 2023-2025 internet and coach phenomenon (originated by the LetsRun user "sirpoc", now James Copeland's 2025 book). The scientific ancestor is the elite "Norwegian model" (lactate-guided threshold intervals), and its own review authors call for controlled studies. Double threshold is not advisable for amateurs without supervision. The singles idea (2-3 controlled sub-threshold sessions a week, everything else easy) is plausible and low risk, but it is coach opinion.

### Cited Findings
- Copeland, *Norwegian Singles Method: Subthreshold Running Kept Simple* (self-published 2025, foreword by Marius Bakken). The author reports going from 28 min to 15:01 for 5K and a 2:24 marathon. This is an anecdote. — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/244528014-norwegian-singles-method)
- Typical prescription across guides: 2-3 sub-threshold sessions a week, just below LT2 with short rests (e.g. 1K, 2K or 3 min reps), easy running on all other days, and often a long run on the weekend. — [RunMastery guide](https://runmastery.co/guides/norwegian-singles/); [The Running Channel](https://therunningchannel.com/norwegian-singles/); [norwegiansingles.run](https://norwegiansingles.run/)
- Search found no peer-reviewed 2025 study of Norwegian singles. A non-academic site argues it may under-develop VO2peak and economy at higher speeds. — [getfitcraft research page](https://getfitcraft.com/science/norwegian-singles-method-research)
- Casado et al. 2023 review: the Norwegian model is lactate-guided threshold intervals (~2.0-4.5 mmol/L) inside high-volume low-intensity training. Controlled studies are needed, and whether double threshold changes injury risk is unclear. — [RunnersTribe](https://runnerstribe.com/features/norwegian-model-unveiling-the-effectiveness-in-endurance-training/); [RunnersConnect double threshold](https://runnersconnect.net/the-science-of-double-threshold-workouts/)
- Coaches advise amateurs to adopt the *intensity control* rather than double days. They warn that age-groupers overestimate their LT2, suggest replacing double sessions with easy running, and flag injury risk in unsupervised double threshold. — [O2 Trening substack](https://o2trening.substack.com/p/the-norwegian-method-for-recreational); [TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/the-norwegian-training-method-simplified-how-to-use-it-at-any-level/); [Triathlete](https://www.triathlete.com/training/what-does-it-mean-to-train-like-a-norwegian?scope=anon)
- Related Published evidence: Festa 2020 found a threshold-heavy "focused" distribution matched polarized results in recreational runners with 17% less time. This is indirect support that more sub-threshold work is not harmful to results. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2019.00070)

### Inferences
- An app can offer a "sub-threshold" plan variant: at 3-4 runs/week, 2 sub-T sessions (e.g. 6×1 km, 4×2 km or 10×3 min at about half-marathon to 15K pace, RPE 6-7/10, 60 s jog) + 1 long easy + optional easy run. Hard guard: the RPE-at-pace drift signal must stay flat. If session RPE exceeds 7, the next session slows ~5 s/km. Without lactate testing, "slightly slower than T pace" (about T + 5-10 s/km) is the safe proxy.
- With only 3 runs/week, 2 sub-T sessions leave little easy running. Offer it only for ≥4 runs/week, or for runners with ≥8 weeks of consistent base.

### Gaps
- No RCT or cohort data on Norwegian singles, or on double threshold in amateurs. The original sirpoc LetsRun thread was not fetched.

## Q5. What plan structures exist for first 5K, faster 5K/10K, and half marathon?

### Takeaway
First 5K: run-walk progressions over about 9 weeks (C25K), which should be made flexible and repeatable given ~73% non-completion. Faster 5K/10K and half marathon: base, then quality phases (Daniels-style), with pace bands from VDOT, 1-2 quality sessions a week, a long run growing under the 30-day cap, and a 1-3 week taper scaled to distance.

### Cited Findings
- C25K: 9 weeks, 3 sessions a week of progressive run-walk intervals. Only 27.3% completed in a UK cohort. Injury, design rigidity and negative emotions drove dropout. The authors recommend flexible designs and injury-prevention content. — [PMC10487403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10487403/)
- Run-walk ratios by pace (Galloway, coach): e.g. slower runners use shorter run segments with 30 s walks. Performance is similar to continuous running with less discomfort (Hottenrott 2016). — [RunnersConnect Galloway interview](https://runnersconnect.net/running-interviews/run-walk-run-with-jeff-and-barbara-galloway/); [BL record](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100029893803_0x000001)
- Taper length scales with event: about 2 weeks is optimal across sports (Bosquet), and about 3 weeks for the marathon (Smyth). — [PubMed 17762369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/); [PMC8506252](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8506252)
- Templates: polarized 77/3/20 or focused 40/50/10 for 10K-oriented recreational runners. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2019.00070)

### Inferences (proposed structures)
- **First 5K (8-12 weeks, 3 sessions):** time-based run-walk. Advance only when the current week felt RPE ≤5. Otherwise repeat the week; the app should frame a repeat positively, not as failure. Final goal: 30 min continuous, or a 5K run-walk.
- **Faster 5K/10K (8-12 weeks, 3-4 runs):** 2-4 weeks base (easy + strides + optional hill sprints), then 4-6 weeks with alternating I (5×3-4 min at I pace) and T (cruise intervals 3-4×6-8 min) sessions, then 1-2 weeks race-specific with a ~1-week taper (volume −30-50%, keep one short quality session).
- **Half marathon (10-16 weeks, 3-4 runs):** build the long run to about 16-20 km under the 30-day cap, mostly T plus HM-pace work, and a 2-week taper at −40-60% volume.

### Gaps
- No trial evidence on strides and hill sprints for recreational runners was retrieved (common coach practice). Specific Pfitzinger and Hansons half-marathon schedules were not primary-sourced.
